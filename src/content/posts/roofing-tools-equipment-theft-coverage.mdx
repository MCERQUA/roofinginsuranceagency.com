## The Tool Trailer Break-In

It's Monday morning. Your crew shows up to a residential job site to find the back of the trailer popped open and cleaned out overnight. Nail guns, two compressors, a generator, ladders — gone. You call your insurance agent. The answer you don't want to hear: "That's not covered under your property policy."

Tool theft from job sites and vehicles is one of the most common claims for roofing contractors — and one of the most frequently under-insured.

## Why Commercial Property Doesn't Cover This

Commercial property insurance covers tools and equipment **at your listed location** — your shop, office, or storage facility. Once tools leave that location and go to a job site, they're typically no longer covered under a standard property policy.

The relevant language is usually a "premises" limitation or an off-premises sublimit that's so low it barely covers a single nail gun. Roofing contractors who've had commercial property for years sometimes discover this gap for the first time after a job-site break-in.

## What Inland Marine (Tools and Equipment) Coverage Does

Inland marine coverage — commonly called tools and equipment or contractor's equipment insurance — is specifically designed to cover portable property **wherever it goes**. Key features:

**Job-site theft coverage.** Tools stolen from an active or inactive job site are covered. This includes tools left on-site overnight or over a weekend.

**In-transit coverage.** Tools damaged or stolen while being transported in a truck or trailer are covered.

**Shop and storage coverage.** Tools at your shop or storage facility are covered, often in addition to your commercial property policy.

**Mysterious disappearance.** On many forms, tools that simply go missing — no evidence of theft, just gone — are covered under mysterious disappearance. This matters because proving theft isn't always possible.

**Rented equipment.** Many policies can be extended to cover rented equipment in your care, custody, or control. If you rent lifts, scaffolding, or generators for specific jobs, ask about adding rented equipment.

## What Typically Isn't Covered

Even with a good tools and equipment policy, watch for these common exclusions or limitations:

- **Breakage of fragile items.** Glass and some electronics may be excluded if damaged (not stolen).
- **Wear and tear / mechanical breakdown.** Tools that stop working due to normal use are not covered — this is a maintenance issue, not an insurable event.
- **Theft from unlocked vehicles.** Many policies require evidence of forced entry to cover theft from a vehicle. If tools are stolen from an unlocked truck, the claim may be denied. Always lock vehicles.
- **Flood damage.** Tools damaged by flooding may be excluded depending on the policy form.

## Building Your Equipment Schedule

Inland marine policies for tools are typically written on a **scheduled** or **blanket** basis:

- **Scheduled:** Each piece of equipment is listed individually with its value. Higher-value items (compressors, generators, specialty tools) are typically scheduled this way.
- **Blanket:** A total coverage limit applies to all tools up to that limit, without individually listing each item. Good for general hand tools and smaller equipment where individual scheduling would be cumbersome.

Most roofing contractors use a combination — blanket coverage for general tools with scheduled coverage for high-value items.

## How Much Coverage Do You Need?

A practical starting point: take an honest inventory of what it would cost to **replace** (not what you paid for) all your tools, nail guns, compressors, generators, ladders, scaffolding, and staging equipment. That replacement cost is your minimum limit.

If you run multiple crews, multiply accordingly — tools for each crew may be at different locations or in different trucks on any given day.

## The Claim Process

Tools and equipment claims are typically handled quickly because the damage is straightforward to document and value. To support a theft claim:

- File a police report (required by most carriers).
- Provide a list of stolen items with model numbers and approximate replacement costs.
- Note whether the vehicle or storage unit was locked (and any evidence of forced entry).

Pre-loss documentation — photos of your equipment, serial numbers, purchase receipts — speeds up claims significantly. Keep this documentation somewhere you can access it even if the equipment is stolen.

## Bottom Line

Roofing tools are expensive, portable, and attractive targets. Standard commercial property won't cover them at job sites.
